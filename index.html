The hard part of enterprise AI is making the output trustworthy.

Engineers adopt AI tools quickly. Getting people to use them is rarely the problem. The problem is making the output reliable enough that an organization can depend on it.

That is a leadership problem before it is a technology problem. This page is my point of view on solving it, along with tools you can use in your own organization.

Four principles I lead by

  • Apply AI where it earns its place

    Not every workflow benefits from AI. I partner with business and technology leaders to decide where it creates real value, and I am willing to say where it does not.

  • Govern the path to production

    AI-generated work should reach production through the same discipline as everything else: a clear spec first, human review in the loop, and automated quality gates that scale as output volume grows.

  • Choose tools by fit, not by hype

    Different models and tools suit different stages of delivery. Tool and model decisions should be deliberate, evaluated, and revisited as the landscape changes.

  • Adoption is a practice, not a mandate

    Teams adopt what makes their work easier. Reusable patterns, clear standards, and hands-on enablement move an organization faster than directives do.

Should this use case get AI?

A quick triage for any proposed AI use case. Answer four questions and get a starting recommendation, plus the guardrails it would need. Use it to open a conversation with your team, not to close one.

If this works, how much does it matter?

Time saved, cost reduced, quality improved, or new capability created.

What happens when the output is wrong?

High means customer, financial, legal, or safety impact.

Is the data ready?

Available, reasonably clean, and approved for this use.

Can a person check the output before it takes effect?

A review step, an approval, or an easy way to undo.

    How I lead people through change

    I set clear expectations, then give teams real ownership. I stay close enough to remove roadblocks, and I communicate decisions and the reasoning behind them openly.

    Most of all, I build the psychological safety that lets someone say "this AI output is wrong" early. That is where trust in AI actually starts.
